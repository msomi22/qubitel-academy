# COMPLETE COSMETOLOGY SCIENCE & PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE

## Comprehensive Student and Educator Course Guide

### Scientific Foundations • Hair • Skin • Nails • Beauty Therapy • Professional Practice • Business • Research

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS COURSE

This course provides a comprehensive theoretical and professional foundation in cosmetology.

It is designed to help a learner understand not only **how cosmetology services are performed**, but also:

* why they work;
* what happens biologically and chemically;
* when a service is appropriate;
* when a service should not be performed;
* how products interact with hair, skin and nails;
* how infections and injuries are prevented;
* how clients are assessed and advised;
* how professional salons are operated;
* how cosmetology claims can be evaluated scientifically.

The curriculum is mapped to the occupational areas appearing in the Kenyan TVET-CDACC Cosmetology Levels 3–6 structure while incorporating subject areas commonly found in international cosmetology and beauty-therapy education.

It is an independent learning and teaching resource and should not be represented as an official TVET-CDACC, CIDESCO, City & Guilds, Milady or other awarding-body qualification unless separately approved by the relevant body.

---

# 2. COURSE PHILOSOPHY

Traditional cosmetology courses are often arranged around services:

> Braiding → manicure → makeup → hair cutting → colouring → massage.

This guide uses a different approach.

The learner first understands the **science**, then applies that science to the service.

For example:

**Trichology**

↓

Hair anatomy and physiology

↓

Keratin and hair-fibre structure

↓

Porosity and elasticity

↓

Hair chemistry

↓

Shampooing and conditioning

↓

Heat styling

↓

Chemical reformation

↓

Hair colouring

This allows students to understand cosmetology instead of memorising procedures.

---

# 3. OVERALL COURSE OUTCOMES

By the end of the complete course, the learner should be able to:

1. Explain the scientific principles underlying professional cosmetology.

2. Describe the anatomy and physiology relevant to hair, skin, nails, massage and beauty therapy.

3. Explain the structure, properties, growth and common abnormalities of hair, scalp, skin and nails.

4. Apply principles of microbiology, infection prevention, hygiene and occupational safety.

5. Explain fundamental chemistry and cosmetic chemistry relevant to professional products.

6. Interpret product ingredients, pH, chemical reactions, formulations and basic safety information.

7. Analyse hair, scalp, skin and nails within the professional scope of a cosmetologist.

8. Distinguish normal conditions from contraindications and conditions requiring referral to an appropriate healthcare professional.

9. Explain the science and theory behind hair cutting, styling, braiding, barbering, extensions, colouring and chemical texture services.

10. Explain the science and theory behind skin care, makeup, hair removal, nail services and body massage.

11. Conduct systematic client consultations and maintain professional client records.

12. Apply professional ethics, communication, customer-care and workplace standards.

13. Apply entrepreneurship, digital literacy and salon-management principles.

14. Evaluate cosmetic claims using basic scientific and research principles.

15. Integrate theory with practical workplace experience and professional decision-making.

# 4. IMPORTANT PROFESSIONAL BOUNDARIES

Cosmetology involves knowledge of conditions affecting hair, skin and nails, but cosmetologists are not automatically medical practitioners.

Therefore this course uses terminology carefully.

### Dermatology

Dermatology is a medical specialty.

Cosmetology students therefore study:

**Skin Science / Cutaneous Biology and Dermatological Concepts for Cosmetology.**

They learn to recognise abnormal findings and contraindications but do not medically diagnose diseases unless separately qualified to do so.

### Trichology

Trichology is the study of the hair and scalp.

The cosmetology learner studies hair biology, scalp conditions, hair loss, fibre properties and their relationship to cosmetic services.

### Onychology

Onychology refers to the scientific study of nails.

Learners study nail anatomy, growth, abnormalities and conditions relevant to professional nail services.

### Referral principle

The professional question should often be:

> “Is it safe and appropriate for me to perform this service?”

rather than:

> “What disease does this client have?”

## Evidence-based cosmetology

Students should learn to investigate claims such as:

“Does this product repair damaged hair?”

“This oil makes hair grow faster.”

“This ingredient removes pigmentation.”

“This treatment permanently closes pores.”

“This cream replaces lost collagen.”

The correct approach is:

**claim → evidence → methodology → results → limitations → conclusion.**

Marketing claims should not automatically be treated as scientific facts.

# 11. QUESTION DESIGN

Questions should increasingly move from:

**Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate.**

For example:

### Remember

Name the three principal structural regions of the hair shaft.

### Understand

Explain the function of the cuticle.

### Apply

A client's hair has very high porosity. Explain how this could influence chemical-colour application.

### Analyse

A client reports repeated bleaching and thermal straightening. Their hair now stretches excessively when wet and breaks easily. Analyse the likely fibre condition and identify professional precautions.

### Evaluate

A manufacturer claims that a shampoo “permanently repairs all damaged hair bonds after one wash.” Explain how you would evaluate this claim scientifically.

# 15. FINAL COURSE OUTCOME

A learner completing this programme should no longer simply know:

> “Use this product for this service.”

The learner should instead be able to reason:

> What is the condition of the client's hair, skin or nails?

> What anatomy and physiology are relevant?

> What product chemistry is involved?

> What reaction or physical change will occur?

> What risks exist?

> Are there contraindications?

> Is this within my professional scope?

> What result should I expect?

> How should I communicate this to the client?

> What evidence supports the product or treatment claim?

> How should the service be documented?

That is the difference between learning a collection of beauty procedures and receiving a genuine **professional cosmetology education**.
