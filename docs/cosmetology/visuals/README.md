# Cosmetology Visual Illustration Standard

> **Instructional visual standard for the Complete Cosmetology Science & Professional Practice course**

Visuals are a required part of the student learning material. They are not decorative additions.

The purpose of every figure is to make a concept easier to understand, identify, compare, remember, or apply safely.

## Core Rule

> **If a structure is named, show it. If a process is described, illustrate it. If a tool, shape, condition, or visual comparison is taught, show the learner what it looks like.**

## When an Illustration Is Mandatory

Create or include an instructional visual when teaching:

- anatomy and named structures;
- internal or cross-sectional structures;
- biological growth cycles;
- chemical or physical processes that benefit from a diagram;
- tools and equipment;
- shapes, angles, patterns, and sectioning;
- service sequences and workflows;
- visible conditions or abnormalities;
- normal-versus-abnormal comparisons;
- safety decisions and contraindications.

## Visual Types

### Labeled anatomy diagrams

Examples:

- nail unit;
- hair follicle;
- hair shaft;
- skin layers;
- facial muscles;
- hand and foot anatomy.

### Process diagrams

Examples:

- hair-growth cycle;
- nail growth;
- keratinisation;
- oxidation;
- polymerisation;
- infection chain.

### Comparison visuals

Examples:

- normal nail vs onycholysis;
- healthy scalp vs inflamed scalp;
- oil-in-water vs water-in-oil emulsion;
- temporary vs permanent colour.

### Tool-identification visuals

Examples:

- manicure tools;
- barbering tools;
- e-file bits;
- facial equipment.

### Technique and geometry visuals

Examples:

- haircutting elevation;
- sectioning;
- nail apex and stress area;
- braid parting;
- face shapes.

### Safety visuals

Examples:

- contraindication decision tree;
- clean/disinfect/sterilise workflow;
- PPE;
- service proceed/modify/refer flow.

## Placement

Place each visual close to the text it explains.

Do not expect learners to navigate to a separate image gallery just to understand a concept.

Example:

```markdown
## Anatomy of the Nail Unit

![Figure 18.1 — Anatomy of the nail unit](../../visuals/04-nails-body/18-01-nail-unit-anatomy.png)

**Figure 18.1:** Major visible and internal structures of the human nail unit.
```

## Figure Naming

Use:

```text
<module>-<figure-number>-<descriptive-name>.<ext>
```

Examples:

```text
07-01-hair-follicle-anatomy.png
14-02-skin-layers-cross-section.png
18-01-nail-unit-anatomy.png
18-04-nail-shapes.png
```

## Figure Numbering

Use the module number followed by a figure sequence.

Examples:

- Figure 7.1
- Figure 7.2
- Figure 18.1
- Figure 18.2

## Quality Standard

Every instructional visual must be:

- factually accurate;
- clearly labeled;
- readable on a phone and desktop;
- suitable for print where possible;
- visually consistent with the rest of the course;
- free from misleading decorative details;
- accessible through useful alt text;
- accompanied by a caption.

## Style

Prefer clean educational illustrations similar to professional anatomy or cosmetology textbooks.

Where appropriate, use:

- a clear white or neutral background;
- strong separation between structures;
- leader lines and readable labels;
- top view plus cross-sectional view where both add value;
- realistic enough anatomy to teach correctly;
- simplified presentation where excessive realism would distract.

## Copyright and Ownership

Do not copy diagrams from copyrighted textbooks.

Use:

- original illustrations;
- commissioned/generated educational illustrations that can be used by the project;
- appropriately licensed open resources where suitable.

When an external visual is used, record its source and licence.

## Drafting Rule

If a visual is required but not yet available, add:

```markdown
> **VISUAL REQUIRED — Figure X.Y:** Describe exactly what the figure must show.
```

No student chapter may be marked **Complete** while a required visual remains unresolved.

## Visual Review Checklist

Before approving a figure, confirm:

- [ ] The scientific labels are correct.
- [ ] All structures mentioned in the surrounding lesson are visible where appropriate.
- [ ] The labels point to the correct structures.
- [ ] Text is readable at normal screen size.
- [ ] The visual adds instructional value.
- [ ] The caption explains what the learner should notice.
- [ ] Alt text is meaningful.
- [ ] The image is original or appropriately licensed.
- [ ] The figure is listed in the visual index.

## Example: Onychology

A chapter teaching nail anatomy should not merely define the parts in prose.

At minimum, it should include a labeled figure showing:

- proximal nail fold;
- eponychium;
- cuticle;
- lunula;
- nail plate;
- lateral nail folds;
- free edge;
- nail matrix;
- nail bed;
- hyponychium.

A top view plus a cross-sectional view is preferred because some structures cannot be understood properly from the surface alone.
